How it works

Two ways back in. Both cost something real.

  1. Choose what gets locked

    Apps, sites, categories. Then hand the key over — the list stops being editable on your side alone.

  2. Pair with your ally

    A friend, a sponsor, a partner, or each other. Six-digit code, no social graph, no accounts to hunt for.

  3. Earn it, or ask for it

    Do the thing you committed to and prove it — a walk, a workout, a page written, a session finished. Or request an unlock and wait for them.

  4. Every tamper is visible

    Protection off, permissions revoked, app removed, device gone quiet — your ally is told, with a timestamp, in seconds.

What your ally can and cannot see

They get a status light, not a window.

  • No screenshots. No browsing history. No message content. Ever.
  • They see: protected yes or no, streak, unlock requests, tamper events.
  • Verification photos stay on your device unless you choose to send one.
  • You can end the link — it takes effect after the cooling-off period, and they're told immediately.

Straight answers

Questions worth asking first.

Can it stop me uninstalling it?

No — and any Android app claiming otherwise for adults is breaking Google Play's rules and will eventually be pulled. What it does instead: removal is instant, visible and reported. You'll know your ally is about to find out before you tap the button. That knowledge is the product.

What if my ally is asleep, or we fall out?

Earning your unlock never needs them — that path is always open. And you can always end the link; it just isn't instant, and they're told.

Do we both have to be locked?

No, but it's the better setup. Mutual mode means you hold each other's key. Same app, one tap at setup.

Is my ally spying on me?

They can't. There is no screen content, no history and no message access in the product at all — not gated behind a setting, just not built.

Where is it available?

Android, worldwide, English at launch. More languages follow the first countries that show up.
